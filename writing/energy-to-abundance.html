Energy → Tokens → Intelligence → Invention → Abundance

Eleven observations from a few months of building with agents, plus a lot of podcasts, videos, and Substacks.

The short version. Data centers use energy and GPUs to make tokens. Models use tokens to make intelligence. Humans, and increasingly agents, use that intelligence to invent. Invention makes abundance. That sequence is how I think about the world, AI, and the future.

These are my observations and learnings. Almost none of it is original to me. Here are the eleven.

Intelligence unlocks invention. Invention unlocks abundance.

1. Intelligence is a stack of multipliers.

We are going to get 1,000x intelligence. Then 1,000,000x. Not from one breakthrough. From stacking gains that are already happening.

Model efficiency. Falling token cost. Parallel agents. Better memory and context. Better algorithms. Intelligence here means useful capacity to do work, and all five of those raise it.

Each one is worth a multiple on its own. They are largely independent, so none of them waits on the other four. Stack all five and 1,000x is the conservative read. Stack them again and a million is a number you can say out loud without embarrassing yourself.

Meanwhile everybody is arguing about whether the new model is four percent better on some eval. Wrong argument. Price is going to zero and capacity is compounding.

So sit with the real question. What becomes possible at 1,000x? At 1,000,000x? Careful with that one. It will blow your mind.

2. The impacts of invention are unfathomable.

First chat. Then code. Now automation and agents. Next is invention, and invention will be 1,000x automation.

Automation makes what we already have cheaper. Invention makes things that did not exist before. Both matter. The second one is much bigger.

Most of the new stuff will be combinatorial, which is exactly what these models are already good at. Drugs. Materials. Recipes. Movies. Products. Take what exists, search the space of combinations, test faster than any human team could.

The question I keep coming back to is whether you can automate the invention process itself. Run the loop. Generate candidates. Test them. Keep what works. Go again. I believe the answer is increasingly yes.

Zuckerberg argues a version of this in The Future Is For Everyone. He wants personal superintelligence in individual hands instead of concentrated inside a few institutions. Same claim from the other direction. The payoff is what gets made that would not otherwise exist.

3. Agents are the great catalyst.

Loops, graphs, and a goal. That is the whole thing. We got promised automation for thirty years and kept getting workflow tools. Agents actually do the work.

I run Claude Code every day. I spent last weekend inside Grok Bot. OpenClaw and Hermes Agent are coming at the same problem from different angles. Soon you buy agents off a shelf for every task, or you wire them together yourself with LLMs, MCPs, and APIs. That is manufacturing engineering. You spend less time writing code and more time designing the line.

Efficiency and productivity are necessary and they are great. That is how costs come down. Costs coming down is what frees everybody up to go invent. Automation is the on-ramp.

4. The product overhang is enormous.

The models are good enough. Go look at Artificial Analysis. Hundreds of them across dozens of labs. Gemini, Claude, ChatGPT, Qwen, xAI, the whole run of Chinese models. Open and closed. Small and cheap, large and expensive.

Almost nothing sits on top of them yet. Tools, agents, harnesses. We are just getting started.

If every lab stopped training tomorrow we would still have a decade of products to build. Anybody telling you the models are not ready is describing their own imagination. The gap between what these things can already do and what anyone has shipped is the biggest opportunity on the board.

Do it now. Do not wait.

5. We are going to build 1,000x more software.

One user. Perfectly customized. Used once and thrown away. Run locally. Spun up instantly. Never reviewed, never shared.

Minimal bells and whistles. Not meant for production. Not meant to persist. All the complexity and the calcs abstracted away so nobody using it ever sees them.

It may not even look like software. Excel is software with a UI so good the coding disappeared, and the formulas sitting on top of the database are very real. Everyone is already a builder. More and more of everything is just code.

Software exists to solve a problem. When the marginal cost hits zero you build the solution and then you delete it.

6. Outcomes > Output. Pay for performance is back.

People will pay for performance. Results matter more than effort. The software underneath stops mattering.

Inside companies this shows up as speed to innovate beating cost savings. Different budget, different buyer.

Do not buy software. Buy results.

7. Vibe-living.

The ultimate luxury, and the natural extension of number six.

You request an outcome and you name the spend. $X in, Y result out. Things get done, built, designed, and fixed.

Underneath it is an auto-combo. Software, robots, and capital assembled on the fly against a verifiable outcome, then taken apart once that outcome is met. A temporary assembly that existed to produce one result.

Vibe coding was the small version. The big version is that you stop specifying how any of it happens. Verification is the whole trick. Define done, prove done, put money against it, and let the system figure out the rest.

Abundance abstracts away the effort and the inputs it takes to get a result. Ozempic for the physical world.

8. Agent routers and model routers will be huge.

There are too many models, agents, and tools to keep up with. Nobody is hand-picking a model per task for much longer. Router companies, or the frontier labs themselves, will do it for us.

Route on capability, task complexity, cost, quality, reliability, data privacy, security, scalability, and repeatability. General model or industry-specific. Cheap and fast for the easy 80 percent, expensive and slow for the rest. That saves a lot of money.

Tools and agents are proliferating the way Zapier recipes did, and they will resolve the same way, into prepackaged workflows that solve one specific thing. Whoever owns that layer owns a lot.

9. Voice finally works.

Voice frickin’ works. Huzzah.

Fast, accurate, and the old bugs are gone. No more fighting the transcript or cleaning up every third word.

I talked most of this piece into Claude Code. That matters more than it sounds like it does. The input method was the bottleneck on how much thinking made it out of my head. That bottleneck is gone.

10. There has never been a better time to start a company.

Everything above stacks into this one. Cheap intelligence. An embarrassing product overhang. Disposable software. Buyers who want outcomes. Routing that keeps the bill down.

One person with a stack of agents covers what used to take a floor of people. Design, build, test, ship, support.

The floor to start has never been lower and the ceiling on a small team has never been higher. More companies. Smaller, stranger, faster. More things that exist because one person could finally make them.

Output is nearly free now. Outcomes are the only thing left worth managing.

11. Speed to power is the limiter.

Every link in that sequence runs on electrons. Energy is what is holding the whole thing back, and it is not close.

Abundance requires very low prices on raw inputs, and the rawest input is power. High price electricity is the single biggest blocker. Cheap electricity is the only path forward. Solar and storage, nuclear, geothermal, wind, SMRs, ten different storage ideas, balcony solar. All of it, and far more of it than anyone is planning for. Building more is also how prices come down, and prices coming down is what massively accelerates the sequence.

The grid has more buffer in it than people assume. The near-term answer is less steel in the ground and more flexibility, orchestration, and getting out of our own way. Solar and batteries. Behind-the-meter gas. Repurposing commercial and industrial sites that already have interconnection. Modular data centers. Data centers overseas. Data centers in space. Anything but standing in the interconnection queue hoping the line moves.

Energized megawatts are the binding constraint on the next decade. Ahead of chips, ahead of capital, ahead of talent. That is the argument I made in Not All Tokens Are Equal.

Most people aren’t ambitious enough about any of this. An abundant future does not arrive on its own. It needs to get built.

The future is for everyone. Mostly it is for the people who start now.
